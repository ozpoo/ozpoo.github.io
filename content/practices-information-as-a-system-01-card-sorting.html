Card Sorting

Finding groups

Information as a System: Practices for investigating information through structure.

Part of The Foundations: An emerging body of research, teaching, and public inquiry into technological literacy and human agency.

What it is

Card sorting is a way of organizing individual pieces of information into groups.

Each piece becomes visible and movable. Instead of deciding on categories first, you can experiment with different ways of grouping the information.

The question

What belongs together?

Works well with

  • People
  • Objects
  • Events
  • Concepts
  • Quotes
  • Images
  • Products
  • Characteristics
  • Responses
  • Observations

Try it

  1. Put each piece of information on its own card.
  2. Spread the cards out so you can see everything.
  3. Look for pieces that seem related.
  4. Create groups.
  5. Give each group a temporary name.
  6. Move cards between groups.
  7. Notice cards that do not fit.
  8. Combine, divide, rename, or remove groups.
  9. Record what changed.

Look for

  • Natural groupings
  • Unexpected connections
  • Ambiguous information
  • Overlapping categories
  • Missing information
  • Items that do not fit

Case study

Investigating a collection of objects

Imagine you are investigating the contents of a household junk drawer.

Instead of organizing the objects immediately by type, place every object on a separate card:

  • battery
  • key
  • rubber band
  • screwdriver
  • receipt
  • USB cable
  • coin
  • pen
  • tape measure
  • button

Begin grouping.

You might create:

Tools
screwdriver / tape measure

Fasteners
rubber band / button

Writing
pen

Technology
USB cable / battery

But another arrangement might group things by frequency of use, material, purpose, or where they are used.

The exercise reveals an important idea:

There is rarely only one possible structure.
