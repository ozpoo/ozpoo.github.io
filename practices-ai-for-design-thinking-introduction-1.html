AI for Design Thinking
Practices for using AI as a partner in design thinking

Introduction

AI as a Design Partner

AI can generate images, write text, summarize information, and produce ideas—but those are capabilities.

The more interesting question for design is:

How can AI help us think?

Design is not simply the production of an answer.

It involves questioning, exploring, comparing, imagining, testing, reflecting, and making decisions.

AI can participate in these activities in different ways:

  • It can help generate possibilities.
  • It can challenge an idea.
  • It can reveal patterns.
  • It can simulate a perspective.
  • It can translate an idea into another form.
  • It can help us notice what we have overlooked.

In each case, the designer remains responsible for the direction, interpretation, and decision-making.

The goal is not to ask AI to design for you.

The goal is to use AI to extend the ways you can think about a design problem.

AI Is a Partner, Not an Authority

AI can produce convincing responses without understanding your project in the way you do.

Its responses can be incomplete, inaccurate, generic, biased, or simply wrong.

Treat its output as material for investigation.

  • Question it.
  • Test it.
  • Push against it.
  • Ask where an idea came from.
  • Ask what it assumes.
  • Ask what it missed.

The useful question is rarely:

Is this AI-generated idea good?

Instead ask:

What does this response help me see, question, or consider?

Choosing a Practice

Different practices support different kinds of thinking.

You can combine practices—a single design problem may benefit from several.

If you want to… Try…
Generate possibilities Brainstorm Partner
Challenge your idea Critique Partner
Find weaknesses Devil’s Advocate
Reframe a problem Metaphor Machine
Identify recurring ideas Pattern Finder
Examine your process Reflection Partner
Investigate a problem Research Partner
Explore possible situations Simulator
Test an idea across language Translator
Rehearse an interaction User Tester
Explore another perspective Audience

Combining Practices

These practices become more useful when they work together.

For example:

Brainstorm Partner
What else could this be?

↓

Metaphor Machine
What else is this like?

↓

Devil’s Advocate
Why might this idea fail?

↓

Critique Partner
What problems do I see now?

↓

User Tester
How might someone encounter it?

↓

Reflection Partner
What did I learn from the process?

The goal is not to use AI at every stage.

The goal is to choose the role that helps you think differently at the moment you need it.

The Designer Remains in the Loop

AI can generate.

AI can compare.

AI can simulate.

AI can question.

AI can translate.

AI can suggest.

But the designer must still:

  • Decide.
  • Interpret.
  • Verify.
  • Test.
  • Take responsibility.

The value of AI in design thinking is not that it removes the need to think.

It is that it can give us more things to think about.

A Final Question

When using AI in your design process, ask:

What did this interaction help me see that I could not—or would not—have seen on my own?

If the answer is nothing, the interaction may not have been useful.

If the answer is something, investigate it.

That is where the design thinking begins.
