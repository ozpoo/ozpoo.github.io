Relationships

Understanding how things relate

Information as a System: Guides for investigating, organizing, and making sense of information.

The question: What kinds of relationships exist between these things?

What it is

A relationship diagram shows connections between pieces of information without requiring a specific spatial, temporal, or hierarchical structure.

It is useful when the relationship itself is the thing you are investigating.

Works well with

  • Concepts
  • Causes and effects
  • Systems
  • Processes
  • Ideas
  • Events
  • Components
  • Abstract information

Try it

  1. Identify the elements.
  2. Place them where they can be easily moved.
  3. Identify possible relationships.
  4. Connect related elements.
  5. Label the connections.
  6. Look for repeated relationship types.
  7. Identify feedback loops or dependencies.
  8. Look for missing relationships.
  9. Revise the diagram.

Look for

  • Cause and effect
  • Dependency
  • Influence
  • Feedback
  • Exchange
  • Conflict
  • Reinforcement
  • Flow

Case study

Investigating a food system

You might begin with:

Farmer → Distributor → Store → Consumer

But further investigation introduces:

Soil → Farmer
Weather → Crop
Transportation → Distributor
Price → Consumer
Consumer demand → Farmer

The diagram becomes increasingly complex.

That complexity is not necessarily a problem.

It may be the thing you are trying to understand.
