Sequence

Understanding process

Information as a System: Guides for investigating, organizing, and making sense of information.

The question: What happens next?

What it is

A sequence organizes information according to order.

Unlike a timeline, sequence does not require dates. It focuses on what happens before, after, or because of something else.

Works well with

  • Processes
  • Instructions
  • User experiences
  • Procedures
  • Workflows
  • Stories
  • Transactions
  • Systems

Try it

  1. Identify the individual steps or events.
  2. Put them in a possible order.
  3. Identify what must happen before something else.
  4. Mark decisions or branching points.
  5. Identify repeated steps.
  6. Look for bottlenecks or unnecessary steps.
  7. Test the sequence against a real example.
  8. Revise.

Look for

  • Dependencies
  • Repetition
  • Delays
  • Decisions
  • Branches
  • Missing steps
  • Points of failure

Case study

Investigating the process of ordering food

A simple sequence might be:

Choose → Order → Pay → Prepare → Wait → Receive → Eat

But investigation may reveal additional steps:

Choose → Customize → Confirm → Pay → Queue → Prepare → Wait → Receive

Each step changes the experience.

Mapping the sequence makes invisible parts of the process visible.
