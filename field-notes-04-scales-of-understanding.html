Field Notes — Scales of Understanding

Field Notes: Working through questions about technology, literacy, systems, and the patterns we use to make sense of the world.

I’ve been thinking about patterns.

A pattern seems like a structure.

A series.

A sequence.

A relationship between things.

Maybe nodes and edges.

Maybe that’s all a pattern really is:

relationships that repeat in some observable way.

And then there’s a model.

Maybe a model is what happens when we recognize a pattern.

A map.

A record.

A representation.

A way of saying:

I think these things are connected.

And maybe understanding is, at least partly, the ability to recognize those patterns and build models from them.

I don’t know.

I’m still working that out.

But there’s something interesting that happens when two models connect.

Or overlap.

Or collide.

Or compliment each other.

Something new seems to happen there.

Is that creativity?

Maybe.

I’ve always had this weird belief that

everything is made of very few things.

Maybe three.

Maybe five.

Maybe some very small number of fundamental things that, through different arrangements and relationships, somehow become everything else.

I’ve never really known what to do with that idea.

Because if I take it literally,

I immediately want to go inward.

Smaller.

Smaller.

Smaller.

Science.

Particles.

Quantum mechanics.

Whatever comes before the thing I thought was the thing.

And eventually I run into the obvious problem:

even if everything is made of a very small number of things,

actually reconstructing everything from those things would be completely impossible.

Not because the fundamental pieces are complicated.

But because the relationships are.

And maybe that’s the point.

Maybe there really are simple subsets.

A few elements.

A few relationships.

But maybe what counts as “simple”

depends on scale.

It’s not necessarily that everything is simple.

Maybe it’s that patterns and relationships allow us to simplify things

at a particular scale.

And suddenly I’m thinking about frameworks.

Because maybe a framework shouldn’t tell you:

Here are the five things that everything is made of.

Maybe that’s too static.

Too tidy.

Too much like a component library.

And that’s not really what I’m trying to do.

I don’t want to define all the scales.

I don’t want to make a taxonomy of every possible level of inquiry.

Because the moment I do that,

I’ve started building the thing I’m trying to avoid.

A fixed system.

A set of prescribed components.

A map that tells you where you are supposed to stand.

Maybe the useful thing isn’t knowing which scale to use.

Maybe it’s knowing that scale exists.

That when you’re struggling with a problem,

you can change the scale of the inquiry.

Zoom out.

Zoom in.

Look at the individual thing.

Look at the relationship between things.

Look at the system containing those things.

Look at the systems containing that system.

Or go the other direction.

Don’t necessarily bring the problem to the scale you already understand.

Bring the scale to the problem.

That feels important.

Maybe the allure of simplicity was actually pointing in the wrong direction.

I was trying to find the few things underneath everything.

Maybe the more useful move is finding the scale at which the thing I’m looking at becomes understandable.

Still figuring it out.
