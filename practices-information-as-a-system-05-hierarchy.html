Hierarchy

Understanding levels

Information as a System: Practices for investigating information through structure.

A part of—The Foundations:
An emerging body of research, teaching, and public inquiry into technological literacy and human agency.

What it is

A hierarchy organizes information according to levels of importance, containment, authority, scale, or dependency.

The question

What is above, below, inside, or dependent on something else?

Works well with

  • Organizations
  • Categories
  • Websites
  • Documents
  • Systems
  • File structures
  • Government
  • Biological systems
  • Complex topics

Try it

  1. Identify the elements.
  2. Identify which elements contain or depend on others.
  3. Find the highest-level element.
  4. Create levels.
  5. Place related elements underneath.
  6. Check for items that could exist at multiple levels.
  7. Test whether the hierarchy communicates the intended relationship.
  8. Revise.

Look for

  • Parent/child relationships
  • Levels
  • Dependencies
  • Primary and secondary information
  • Missing levels
  • Ambiguous placement

Case study

Investigating a university

You might begin with:

University

→ Colleges
→ Departments
→ Programs
→ Courses
→ Sections

But a student does not experience the university only through this hierarchy.

They may also move through:

University → Campus → Building → Room

or:

University → Course → Assignment → Submission

The hierarchy you choose depends on the question you are investigating.
