Field Notes — Strange Loops

Field Notes: Working through questions about technology, literacy, systems, and the patterns we use to make sense of the world.

I’ve started noticing something strange about the way I think.

I’ll get an idea.

Something catches.

A question.

A pattern.

A possibility.

And then I chase it.

Sometimes for hours.

Sometimes for days.

I’ll follow it somewhere completely unexpected.

I’ll pull it apart.

Turn it around.

Try another perspective.

Build a model.

Break the model.

Build another one.

And eventually—

I end up right back where I started.

Except it doesn’t feel like where I started.

That’s the strange part.

Looking backward, I can sometimes see that the thing I “discovered” was already part of how I understood the world.

It was already in my model.

I just couldn’t see it from where I was standing.

So I walked around it.

And when I came back, I could see it differently.

Maybe deeper.

Maybe more clearly.

Maybe just from another angle.

I’m not sure.

I do this constantly when I program.

I’ll reverse engineer something.

Rebuild it.

Then rebuild it again.

And again.

And somehow, I’ll arrive at essentially the same outcome each time.

But something has changed.

The result is similar.

The understanding isn’t.

The system feels different.

Cleaner.

More coherent.

More mine.

And I keep doing it until something tells me to stop.

I don’t actually know what tells me to stop.

That’s another question.

Maybe learning isn’t always moving forward.

Maybe sometimes it’s moving around.

Maybe understanding isn’t something we simply acquire.

Maybe it is something we repeatedly encounter from different positions.

Experience.

Question.

Wandering.

Pattern.

Model.

Testing.

Return.

And then—

another question.

I’ve been thinking a lot about awareness lately.

Just paying attention.

Listening to myself.

Noticing the things that keep appearing.

A thought that won’t leave.

A pattern I keep seeing.

Something that catches my attention over and over again.

I’m beginning to wonder whether those repetitions matter.

Not because every recurring thought is meaningful.

But because repeated attention creates something.

A kind of internal map.

A collection of experiences, patterns, and relationships that I might not consciously know how to describe.

And then something happens.

An event.

An idea.

A conversation.

A breakthrough.

And suddenly one of those old thoughts comes back.

And it feels like:

Oh.

There you are.

Not a new idea.

A connection.

This reminds me of Westworld.

The maze.

A recursive journey through the same kinds of choices.

Closer.

Further away.

Closer again.

And then there’s that other detail I’ve been thinking about:

The hosts talk to themselves.

Even when nobody is there.

Bernard describes it as error correction.

Practice.

Repetition.

The system rehearsing itself.

I don’t know why that keeps sticking with me.

Maybe because I’ve started wondering if that’s part of what thinking is.

Not just producing new thoughts.

But repeatedly running through experience.

Rehearsing.

Testing.

Comparing.

Correcting.

Returning.

Until something connects.

Could self-awareness work the same way?

Could paying attention to your own experience create a kind of subconscious coach?

Not an answer machine.

Not intuition as some magical thing.

Just accumulated experience becoming available as another layer of context.

Something that notices:

I’ve seen something like this before.

This feels familiar.

There’s a relationship here.

Keep looking.

Maybe learning isn’t just putting new information into a system.

Maybe it is also building the internal conditions that allow future information to connect.

And maybe that’s why I keep going in circles.

Maybe the circle isn’t the failure to move forward.

Maybe the circle is the mechanism.

I don’t know.

But I’m beginning to wonder if the strange part isn’t that I keep arriving back where I started.

Maybe the strange part is that I never arrive as the same person who left.

Still figuring it out.
