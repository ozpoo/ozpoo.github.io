Understanding change

Information as a System: Guides for investigating, organizing, and making sense of information.

The question: What happened, and when?

What it is

A timeline organizes information according to time.

It can reveal sequence, duration, periods of change, gaps, clusters, and relationships between events.

Works well with

  • Historical events
  • Biographies
  • Projects
  • Processes
  • Developments
  • Cultural changes
  • Product histories
  • Personal experiences

Try it

  1. Identify the events or moments.
  2. Record the relevant dates.
  3. Put everything in chronological order.
  4. Mark periods of activity or change.
  5. Look for gaps and clusters.
  6. Identify events that overlap.
  7. Add important relationships or causes.
  8. Ask what the timeline makes visible.

Look for

  • Turning points
  • Periods of rapid change
  • Long periods of stability
  • Gaps
  • Recurring events
  • Overlapping events
  • Before-and-after relationships

Case study

Investigating the history of a technology

Suppose you are investigating the development of the smartphone.

You could collect:

  • Major product releases
  • Operating system developments
  • Network changes
  • Hardware innovations
  • Cultural events
  • Changes in user behavior

A simple timeline may reveal that technological development, infrastructure, and cultural adoption did not happen at the same pace.

The timeline becomes more than a list of dates.

It becomes a way to investigate change.
