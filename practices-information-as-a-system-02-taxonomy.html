Taxonomy

Building categories

Information as a System: Practices for investigating information through structure.

What it is

A taxonomy organizes information into categories and subcategories.

Unlike simple grouping, taxonomy asks how categories relate to one another and whether the same organizing principle is being used consistently.

Works well with

  • Objects
  • Products
  • Species
  • Concepts
  • Organizations
  • Topics
  • Collections
  • Content libraries

The question

How can these things be organized into meaningful categories?

Try it

  1. Identify the things being organized.
  2. List characteristics they have in common.
  3. Choose a meaningful characteristic.
  4. Create broad categories.
  5. Divide categories into subcategories.
  6. Check whether the categories use a consistent logic.
  7. Look for items that could belong in more than one place.
  8. Revise the structure.
  9. Test it with unfamiliar examples.

Look for

  • Categories that overlap
  • Categories that are too broad
  • Categories that are too specific
  • Missing categories
  • Inconsistent logic
  • Exceptions

Case study

Organizing a collection of books

A collection of books could be organized by:

Subject
History → Art History → Modern Art

or by:

Format
Hardcover → Paperback → Digital

or by:

Audience
Children → Young Adult → Adult

Each produces a different structure.

The important question is not:

“Which category is correct?”

It is:

“Which structure helps the intended audience understand and use the collection?”
