Comparison

Understanding difference

Information as a System: Guides for investigating, organizing, and making sense of information.

The question: How are these things similar and different?

What it is

Comparison places two or more things alongside one another to identify similarities, differences, patterns, and meaningful distinctions.

Works well with

  • Products
  • People
  • Places
  • Events
  • Systems
  • Concepts
  • Images
  • Data sets
  • Versions

Try it

  1. Choose the things you want to compare.
  2. Identify characteristics they share.
  3. Identify meaningful differences.
  4. Create consistent criteria.
  5. Place the information side by side.
  6. Look for patterns.
  7. Ask which differences actually matter.
  8. Revise your criteria.

Look for

  • Shared characteristics
  • Significant differences
  • Trade-offs
  • Patterns
  • Outliers
  • Criteria that change the comparison

Case study

Comparing transportation options

You might compare:

  • Bicycle
  • Bus
  • Car
  • Train

Using:

  • Cost
  • Speed
  • Distance
  • Capacity
  • Energy use
  • Accessibility

The comparison creates a structure for understanding the differences.

But changing the criteria changes the story.

That is part of the investigation.
