Network

Understanding connections

Information as a System: Guides for investigating, organizing, and making sense of information.

The question: What is connected to what?

What it is

A network shows individual elements and the connections between them.

Unlike a hierarchy, a network does not assume that everything belongs in a single chain of levels.

Works well with

  • People
  • Organizations
  • Social relationships
  • Transportation
  • Websites
  • Communication
  • Dependencies
  • Information flows
  • Systems

Try it

  1. Identify the elements.
  2. Represent each element as a node.
  3. Identify meaningful connections.
  4. Draw connections between nodes.
  5. Label the connections when useful.
  6. Look for highly connected nodes.
  7. Look for isolated nodes.
  8. Look for clusters.
  9. Ask what the pattern reveals.

Look for

  • Hubs
  • Clusters
  • Isolated elements
  • Strong connections
  • Weak connections
  • Paths
  • Dependencies

Case study

Investigating a social network

Imagine mapping the people involved in organizing an event.

Instead of organizing them by title, connect people according to who communicates with whom.

The result may reveal:

  • A person connecting several groups
  • Groups that rarely communicate
  • Individuals with many connections
  • People who appear central but have little direct interaction

The network reveals relationships that a hierarchy might hide.
