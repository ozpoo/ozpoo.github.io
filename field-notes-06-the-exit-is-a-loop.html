Field Notes — The Exit Is a Loop

Field Notes: Working through questions about technology, literacy, systems, and the patterns we use to make sense of the world.

What if learning isn’t gaining something new?

What if it’s becoming aware?

I keep thinking about this.

We talk about learning as though information gets added to us.

We learn something.

We know something.

We understand something.

As if the mind is a container, and learning means putting another thing inside it.

But maybe that’s not quite what happens.

Maybe the world is already full of things we aren’t capable of seeing.

Maybe learning changes the filter.

Maybe it changes the structure of the observer.

Maybe something happens inside us that allows us to notice something that was already there.

The information wasn’t necessarily added.

The world became more legible.

I think about this a lot as a designer.

Someone who doesn’t understand typography sees text.

A designer might see hierarchy.

Rhythm.

Weight.

Spacing.

Alignment.

Contrast.

System.

The information was always there.

Something changed in the observer.

And suddenly there was more to see.

So maybe learning isn’t always about gaining knowledge.

Maybe it’s about becoming capable of perceiving relationships that were previously invisible.

I’ve been thinking about this because of something that happened to me.

During a particularly painful period of trying to understand myself, I ended up outside, by a river.

I was lying on the ground, shirt off, on the rocks in the sun.

I was trying to connect with something beyond myself.

At the end of that connection, I heard:

Start the exit.

I didn’t know what that meant.

I didn’t have a map.

I didn’t know where the exit was.

But I started.

I made decisions.

I asked questions.

I listened.

I moved.

And as I went through it, I started noticing things.

About myself.

About my work.

About emotion.

About things I had apparently known for a long time without really knowing that I knew them.

Things became visible.

Things connected.

Something shifted.

For a while, it felt like I had found something.

And then, over the past few days, it started to feel like I was losing it.

I wondered if I was doing something wrong.

If I had somehow moved backward.

If the understanding was disappearing.

And then I started thinking about loops.

The maze.

The exit.

My own tendency toward recursion.

The strange way I keep returning to the same questions from different directions.

And I wondered:

What if the exit wasn’t one way?

What if the exit was another loop?

Maybe I didn’t leave the maze.

Maybe I changed how I moved through it.

I made decisions.

Those decisions changed my experience.

My experience changed what I could see.

What I could see changed the decisions I could make.

And eventually I arrived somewhere that looked strangely familiar.

Back where I started.

Except I wasn’t quite the same observer.

Maybe that’s what the exit was.

Not escape.

Reconfiguration.

I keep coming back to nature.

At one point I thought:

She contains all the secrets.

If you’re looking for an answer, you can find it by observing her.

I don’t know if I mean that literally.

But I find something comforting in it.

Nature doesn’t seem particularly interested in explaining itself.

It repeats.

It transforms.

It branches.

It decays.

It grows.

It loops.

It responds.

It recombines.

Patterns appear.

Patterns disappear.

Things become other things.

Maybe we’re doing something similar.

Maybe we’re not creating reality so much as observing it, reflecting it, recombining what we’ve encountered.

Small reflections.

Incomplete reflections.

Different arrangements of the same materials.

Different experiences of the same world.

And maybe the detail of the reflection depends on the awareness of the observer.

Which brings me back to learning.

Do we ever really gain knowledge?

Maybe.

But I’m starting to wonder if knowledge is less like an object we possess and more like a relationship we enter.

Something happens between the world and the observer.

Between experience and memory.

Between memory and interpretation.

Between interpretation and action.

Knowledge isn’t just what is inside us.

It is what becomes visible from where we are standing.

And maybe that means two people can inhabit the same world and experience radically different realities without either of them necessarily having access to more reality than the other.

They are simply positioned differently within it.

Different experiences.

Different histories.

Different filters.

Different intersections.

Different things becoming visible.

I’ve started visualizing this as threads.

I don’t know what the image means yet.

I don’t know if it means anything.

But I keep seeing it.

All that was.

All that is.

All that might be.

Not necessarily as separate worlds, but as an enormous medium of possibility.

And our experience moves along one thread.

Forward.

Backward.

Up.

Down.

Bending.

Returning.

Maybe there are countless other threads moving through the same medium.

And sometimes they intersect.

Sometimes they overlap.

Sometimes a pattern appears because several trajectories happen to meet in the same place.

Maybe that’s where understanding lives.

At the intersections.

Not inside one thread.

Not inside one person.

But in the relationship between things.

And maybe the difference between knowing and not knowing isn’t whether the pattern exists.

Maybe the pattern was already there.

The difference is whether we are currently positioned to see it.

Maybe that’s what learning does.

It doesn’t necessarily put more of the world inside us.

It changes where we stand.

It changes what relationships we can recognize.

It changes which patterns become visible.

It changes which decisions become possible.

And those decisions change where we stand next.

So we move.

We observe.

We reflect.

We decide.

We move again.

A loop.

And maybe every time we think we’ve found the exit, we’re actually finding another way through.

Maybe there was never an exit from the maze.

Maybe the exit was learning how to see the maze.

And maybe, once you can see it,

you can choose where to go next.

Still figuring it out.
