Field Notes
Strangs Loops

Field Notes: Working through questions about technology, literacy, systems, and the patterns we use to make sense of the world.

I do a lot of programming.

I’ll reverse engineer something.

Take it apart.

Rebuild it.

Then rebuild it again.

And again.

And again.

And somehow I’ll end up with essentially the exact same outcome.

But it feels different.

It is different.

I understand it differently.

The structure is cleaner.

The relationships make more sense.

Something has shifted.

And I will keep doing it until—

well.

I don’t actually know what prompts me to stop.

That’s another thing I probably should figure out.

But there’s something else bothering me about this.

I’ve been noticing these inner monologues I have.

These threads I chase down.

Sometimes with what can only be described as fever.

I’ll find some idea.

And I’ll follow it.

And follow it.

And eventually—

I end up right back where I started.

At something I already knew.

And yet when I get there,

it feels different.

Which is strange.

Because if I arrived at the same conclusion,

what changed?

Maybe the model changed.

Maybe I didn’t learn a new fact.

Maybe I built a different relationship between the facts I already had.

I saw the same thing

from another direction.

And somehow that matters.

Looking back at my previous notes, I can see it happening again.

Everything I was describing—

patterns,

relationships,

changing scale,

reconstructing systems,

building models,

connecting seemingly unrelated things—

I already know how to do this.

It’s already how I work.

It’s already embedded in the way I’ve been thinking about literacy.

I didn’t discover some entirely new idea.

I found my way back to an existing one

through a completely different path.

And now I can see it from somewhere else.

Maybe that’s what the wandering is doing.

Maybe the point isn’t always to arrive somewhere new.

Maybe sometimes the point is to arrive at the same place

with a different model of how you got there.

And then—

oh shit.

Back to Westworld.

I’ve been thinking about the Maze again.

The recursive cycle.

The hosts repeating decisions.

Moving through the same structures over and over.

Each decision potentially taking them closer to the center

or further away.

And then there’s Bernard talking about why the hosts speak so much,

even when there are no guests around.

They’re practicing.

Error correction.

Repeating.

Testing.

Refining.

Trying again.

I can’t help but see the connection.

I’ve been on this journey of trying to know myself better.

Part of that has mostly meant

listening.

Observing.

Trying to notice.

Trying to be aware of what keeps appearing.

And I’ll notice a pattern.

Something that sticks out.

Something I keep thinking about.

Something that keeps showing up in slightly different forms.

And I don’t necessarily know why it matters.

I just don’t let it go.

Or maybe it doesn’t let me go.

And then eventually something happens.

An event.

An idea.

An understanding.

A breakthrough.

And suddenly one of those old thoughts comes back into the light.

And it feels almost like it says:

Yup.

There it is.

Which makes me wonder about learning.

Could a deeper awareness of yourself actually become part of the learning process?

Not in some mystical sense.

Just—

if you’re constantly accumulating experiences,

observations,

questions,

failed attempts,

things you’ve noticed,

things you’ve tried,

patterns you’ve encountered—

then maybe some of that becomes a kind of internal model.

A groundwork.

Something you don’t consciously consult,

but that is nevertheless there.

And when you encounter something new,

maybe that model helps you recognize a relationship.

I’ve seen something like this before.

Not the same thing.

Not the same answer.

Just—

a pattern.

Maybe that’s what those strange loops of thinking are doing.

Maybe I’m not going in circles.

Maybe I’m changing the angle.

Maybe I’m testing the same idea against different experiences.

Maybe I’m building and rebuilding the model until something clicks.

And maybe that feeling of:

I’ve somehow ended up exactly where I started,

isn’t failure.

Maybe it’s evidence that something changed.

I just haven’t figured out how to see the change yet.

And that makes me wonder whether literacy might have something to do with this too.

Not just having a collection of knowledge.

Not even just knowing how to ask questions.

But developing enough awareness of your own patterns

that when you encounter something unfamiliar,

you have something inside yourself to connect it to.

A personal model.

A history of trying.

A record of mistakes.

A sense of:

I’ve figured out something like this before.

Maybe that becomes its own kind of tool.

Maybe even its own kind of confidence.

Not:

I know this.

But:

I know how I tend to figure things out.

And maybe that’s a very different kind of knowing.

I don’t know.

That’s probably enough for one drive to work.

But I keep thinking about that distinction.

Maybe learning isn’t always about moving from

not knowing

to

knowing.

Maybe sometimes it’s moving from one model

to another.

And sometimes—

you have to go all the way around

to see that you’ve changed.

Still figuring it out.
